# 16. Calculus I Review

Calculus I is organized around two complementary questions:

1. How fast is a quantity changing now?
2. How much change has accumulated over an interval?

Limits make both questions precise. Derivatives answer the first; integrals
answer the second; the Fundamental Theorem connects them.

## 16.1 Limits

You should be able to:

- estimate one-sided and two-sided limits from graphs and tables;
- separate a limit from a function value;
- apply limit laws after checking that they are valid;
- resolve removable indeterminate forms;
- explain an $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ statement;
- identify continuity and use the Intermediate Value Theorem; and
- analyze infinite limits and limits at infinity.

## 16.2 Derivatives

Connect all three interpretations:

$$
f'(a)
=
\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}
=
\text{tangent slope}
=
\text{instantaneous rate}.
$$

You should be able to:

- choose differentiation rules from expression structure;
- differentiate implicit, inverse, logarithmic, and trigonometric relations;
- interpret first and second derivatives;
- apply the Mean Value Theorem after checking its hypotheses;
- model related rates;
- find local and absolute extrema; and
- build and justify an optimization model.

## 16.3 Integrals

Connect finite sums, antiderivatives, and accumulation:

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i^*)\Delta x
=
F(b)-F(a).
$$

You should be able to:

- distinguish indefinite and definite integrals;
- interpret signed area, displacement, and total distance;
- use integral properties and units;
- apply both parts of the Fundamental Theorem;
- recognize substitution as the reverse chain rule; and
- construct area and volume integrals from representative slices.

## 16.4 A method-selection routine

For any unfamiliar problem:

1. Name the requested quantity and its units.
2. Identify the available representation: graph, table, formula, or context.
3. State the relevant definition or theorem and check its conditions.
4. Build the mathematical expression before calculating.
5. Check signs, units, domain, and whether the result answers the original
   question.

## 16.5 Connections worth explaining

Practice explaining these links without relying on a formula sheet:

- why differentiability implies continuity;
- why the sign of $f'$ controls increase and decrease;
- why the sign of $f''$ controls concavity;
- why an antiderivative includes $+C$ but a definite integral does not;
- why velocity integrates to displacement rather than automatically to
  distance; and
- why the derivative of an accumulation function returns its integrand.

## 16.6 Final perspective

Calculus is not a catalog of symbolic techniques. It is a language for
describing local change, global accumulation, and the relationship between
them. A successful solution makes that meaning visible alongside the
calculation.
